The purpose of this memo is to update the board on another disciplinary grievance submitted by a custodian pursuant to the grievance procedure contained in the collective bargaining agreement between the District and the custodial bargaining unit ("CBA").
This situation is similar to the situation in February 2025 where the board was advised to forego any action at Level III of the custodian grievance procedure in regard to custodian/bus driver.
The custodian has filed a grievance arising out of discipline in the form of a disciplinary letter of reprimand and a disciplinary suspension without pay. The grievance has progressed
1
through the CBA's grievance procedure to Level III . A Level III grievance in the custodial CBA
can involve the board, if the board so chooses. As in the earlier matter in February 2025, we again recommend that the board not become involved in this custodial grievance.
Level III of the grievance procedure contained in the custodial CBA provides:
"Any service employee who has not received a satisfactory settlement as outlined in the previous level [of the grievance procedure] shall submit a grievance in writing to the
School Board. The School Board shall have a hearing and move on the grievance or refer the matter on to the next step [arbitration] within fifteen (15) days. In the event the Board does decide to have a hearing, it shall give a fair and impartial hearing to the aggrieved, along with the supervisor or administrator. The School Board shall render a written decision within fifteen (15) days thereafter. If the School Board's decision is not acceptable to the service employee involved and the matter pertains to this Agreement the matter may proceed to the next step [arbitration] within five (5) days thereafter otherwise the decision of the School Board is final." (Emphasis added).
In summary, if a custodial employee/grievant is unhappy with the resolution of their grievance at Levels I or II of the grievance procedure, Level III requires submission of the grievance to the board. The board then has the option a) to refer the matter on to the next step, which is arbitration, without any action; or b) conduct a hearing before the board on the

Contrary to the statement in Mr. Schmitz email to the board (See Footnote 1 herein), a custodial grievant is not "entitled" to meet with the board. The grievance procedure is clear that the board's involvement in a custodial grievance at Level III is optional and not required.
It is our recommendation that the board decline to hold a hearing on grievance and instead, refer the grievance on to arbitration based upon the following:
1. Involving the board in a grievance procedure is outside the purview of board duties. A
board's role is to enact policy. It is the duty of the superintendent (and superintendent's designees) to handle administrative matters, including employee discipline. Adjustment of grievance is typically beyond the scope of its duties.
2. If the board becomes involved, it will require at least one special board meeting pursuant to the Open Meeting Law. If the board decides to hold a hearing, it will be subject to the provisions of the Minnesota Open Meeting Law, Minn. Stat. Chap. 13D meaning that the hearing will have to be noticed as a special meeting. The board will likely not want to conduct a grievance hearing during a regular board meeting.
3. Board involvement at Level III of the grievance procedure is not simply an opportunity to be heard but requires evidentiary hearing. Based upon our reading of the custodial grievance procedure, the hearing contemplated is not simply an opportunity for the
2
grievant(s) to address the board . Instead, the board's involvement requires a formal evidentiary hearing with witnesses and submission of exhibits. This is clearly not the efficient, cost effective process suggested by the union. Conducting an evidentiary hearing raises questions about who will preside at the hearing, respond to evidentiary objections, admit exhibits into evidence and prepare the board's decision.
4. If the board conducts a hearing, it gives "two bites of the apple" and the matter may still not be completed. If the board decides to hold its own hearing and denies the grievance, it is giving the grievant(s) and the union "two bites of the apple" and presents an opportunity for the grievant and the union to learn the district's strategy in advance of arbitration. Additionally, if the board conducts a Level III hearing and denies the grievance, the matter is not completed because the grievant will then have the right to a second hearing in the form of arbitration. A grievance procedure is intended to allow the parties to resolve a dispute in a prompt and expeditious manner. Conducting two (2)

5. Contrary to the union's Level III request, a hearing conducted by the board is neither efficient nor cost effective. If the board decides to conduct a hearing at Level III it will likely require legal counsel to represent the district plus the time and resources that will be expended by administration to prepare for the hearing. If administration is successful and the board denies the grievance and the matter proceeds to arbitration, the expenditure of time and money, including legal fees (and half the cost of the arbitrator) will arise again when the parties end up in arbitration.
Because of the many detriments to the board in conducting a grievance hearing at Level
III of the custodial grievance procedure, we recommend that the board declines the union's request for a hearing refers the matter on to arbitration.
