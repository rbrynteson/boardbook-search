District 281 Enrollment Analysis: Oct 2025 to Oct 2026
Summary
Closure group: Noble, Sonnesyn and the three elementaries that received their students retained 86.1% of K-4 students, against 98.4% at schools with no closure. That gap is about 143
students.
Lakeview: it is the clear outlier, with 77.8% retention against the students it expected to enroll.
Cooper High School: it retained 87.8% of grades 9-11, against 96.3% at Armstrong, an excess loss of about 82 students.
Largest drop: the 5th grade class lost 118 students (16.5%) on entering 6th grade.
Estimated excess loss: about 225 students, roughly 37% of the 612 students, identified as at risk from the transportation reduction.
Data note: the 2026 sheet's Year GAIN/LOSS row shows -157, but the actual change between the two
October counts is -780. This analysis uses the actual counts, and the row should be checked.

Grade 12 increase: it comes mostly from Robbinsdale Academy-HVW, where grade 11 (33)

5th-to-6th grade drop: it is the largest in the district and lines up with the middle school consolidation and the Robbinsdale Middle School transportation risk.

Elementary schools tied to closure retained about 12 points fewer students than schools with no closure. For elementary, I tracked K-4 students in 2025 to grades 1-5 in 2026, which excludes 5th graders who leave elementary anyway.

Lakeview is the clear outlier. It absorbed the most Noble students, and Noble's 48 transportation-risk students would explain most of its shortfall of about 67 students. Neill held up well, and Zachary is moderately below baseline.
Middle school
The four feeder schools (Fair-Crystal, Plymouth, Robbinsdale, Sandburg) retained 92.8% of their grade 6-
7 students into grades 7-8, or 1,294 of 1,395. That is only slightly worse than the district's own 6-to-7 (-
7.3%) and 7-to-8 (-5.3%) rates, so the real hit is at the 5th-to-6th grade entry point.
Sandburg grew from 306 to 992 (+686), which is more than Robbinsdale Middle School's 571, so some
Fair-Crystal students probably landed there too. Plymouth also rose from 749 to 895.

3. Transportation reduction: did the risk materialize?
The clearest effect is at Cooper High School, and the closure-group elementary loss is the likeliest second effect. Estimated excess loss is about 225 students (143 in the elementary closure group plus 82
at Cooper), against 612 students identified at risk. That is roughly 37%, before any middle school effect.

Caveats
These are retention comparisons, not proof of cause, so the closure and transportation effects cannot be fully separated from ordinary mobility.
What counts as loss: retention is cohort-based, so students who moved, or who enrolled in charter, open-enrollment or private schools, all show up as loss.
Controls: Armstrong is a decent but imperfect control for Cooper, and the six no-closure elementaries are a reasonable control for the closure group.
Reassignment counts: the 186 reassigned from Noble and 175 from Sonnesyn are close to those schools' K-4 enrollments (180 and 210). Sonnesyn's 35-student gap suggests it lost families before reassignment.
