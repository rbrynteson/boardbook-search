1. The geographic area and population to be served, a. preschool through grade 12 student enrollments for the past five years, and b. student enrollment projections for the next five years.
Geographic Area to be served
Independent School District #281 is located approximately 10 miles northwest of the
Minneapolis area. Robbinsdale Area School District serves the cities of Robbinsdale,
New Hope, Crystal, Plymouth, Golden Valley, Brooklyn Center and Brooklyn Park. The district covers an area of 28 square miles and is in Hennepin County which contains approximately 1,280,000 residents
There are approximately 12,000 students in programs from pre-kindergarten through grade 12 housed in 19 buildings. Cooper High School, Robbinsdale Spanish Immersion
(RSI) at Sunny Hollow, Sonnesyn Elementary, New Hope Learning Center, ESC, and
Meadow Lake Elementary are located in New Hope. Armstrong High School, Pilgrim
Lane Elementary, Zachary Lane Elementary, Plymouth Middle School are located in
Plymouth. Forest Elementary, FAIR School Crystal and Neill Elementary are located in
Crystal. Lakeview Elementary and Robbinsdale Middle School are located in
Robbinsdale. Noble Elementary, Sandburg Middle School and School of Engineering and Arts (SEA) are located in Golden Valley. Northport Elementary is located in Brooklyn
Center.
Neighboring school districts with contiguous borders include Minneapolis, Osseo,
Hopkins, Wayzata and Brooklyn Center.

Available Alternate Facilities
Aside from the 19 school buildings there is a bus garage that is 48,843 square feet. It was built in 1966. The District also leases warehouse space for district storage, and commercial space to house the Crystal Learning Center.
Based on the demographic projections and the district's ongoing operational budget challenges, the district is looking to consolidate and reduce its facilities footprint in an effort to increase efficiency and utilization.

Need for renovation
When meeting with district leadership, community members, and interviewing staff, the following items rose to the top of the list to correct:
1. Robbinsdale operates more buildings than needed
• The district has declining enrollment
• Since 1971, enrollment has dropped from 28,000 to just over 10,000
• Since 2009, enrollment has dropped by 2,000 students
• Utilization rates in the schools are very low
• Listed in the previous section are the schools planned for closure for the 2026-
2027 school year
• For efficiency and cost savings, more schools need to be closed
• The plan is to move from two high schools to one high school, to have fewer middle schools, and fewer elementary schools
• To close more schools, small additions to the other sites are needed to house the additional students
2. Facilities are Outdated and Costly to Maintain
• Need for building, technology, and materials to support 21st century learning
• District facilities are outdated and no longer meet current or future needs
• Updated facilities don't just support effective learning, but promote pride and belonging: two things every one of our students deserve
• Without further investment, Robbinsdale Area Schools is at risk of falling further behind our main competitors in the preK-12 enrollment marketplace
3. Safety and Security:
• Thirteen of the Robbinsdale Area School sites do not meet standards for a fully secure building
• The percentage of students that feel safe in the restrooms has dropped significantly
• Need for secure entries
• Updates to restrooms for private restrooms with proper ADA access
• Need for better access and flow
• Need for safety improvements around buildings

Impact on Facility Operating Costs
For the 2025-2026 school year the District operated 20 buildings for instruction and district administration. As a result of the closures already made by the school board and the additional closures resulting from this referendum, the plan is for the District to operate 12 buildings for instruction and administration.
The District closed Sonnesyn Elementary, Noble Elementary, Robbinsdale Middle School, and the Education Services Center (ESC) for the 2026-2027 school year, as well as the discontinuation of the middle school program at FAIR School-Crystal. Students will be shifted to the remaining buildings and the ESC is moving to FAIR Crystal temporarily. The resulting operational savings from those closures will be approximately $3,800,000 annually.
Once fully implemented, the estimated impact of this referendum on annual facility operating costs is a further reduction of $5,000,000 because of the closure of one high school and three elementary/early childhood schools. These savings are a key component in the District's statutory operating debt (SOD) recovery plan.
Project Schedule
If the referendum is successful, project design would begin in November of 2026, with initial bidding taking place in April of 2027. Phased construction would commence the summer of
2027, with the entire project being completed by end of summer 2031. Due to this elongated construction period, a construction inflation allowance of 12% has been built into the budgets.
