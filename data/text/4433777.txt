Reading
Where we were
It is important to look at the growth that students are making according to the screening data. Within the district, more students in kindergarten and first grade are showing typical growth than would be expected from national growth norms. Kindergarten has shown a large increase in proficiency levels, while first grade remained relatively steady from fall to winter. In second grade, students held relatively consistent proficiency levels and national growth norms. In grades three through five, fewer students are showing typical growth in comparison to national norms. In third, fourth, and fifth grade, the proficiency levels of students are decreasing from fall to winter.
Where we are
The primary focus of professional learning in the last few years was Volume 1 of LETRS which focuses on the foundational skills of phonemic awareness and phonics. As a result of this training:
● The growth and proficiency levels shown in kindergarten and first grade have increased.
● A restructured scope and sequence in kindergarten and first grade was implemented to accelerate the learning of foundational literacy skills.
● Five of the elementary school sites (ENE, LVE, NOE, RSI, and SOE) are implementing a structured literacy curriculum entitled Bridge 2 Read (B2R) in kindergarten through third grade, funded by a grant from the Ciresi Walburn Foundation. B2R provides explicit instruction in phonemic awareness, phonics, and fluency.
RAS employs a variety of reading interventions to meet the needs of students:
● PRESS Interventions
● Lexia Core 5
● Aspire units
● FASTBridge interventions
● WIN time interventions
Where we are going
Following the READ Act legislation, ongoing professional learning in the Science of Reading will continue to meet the needs of the students and teachers.
● RAS is working with MDE to establish a professional learning plan for the coming years that will train all of the elementary classroom teachers, Multilingual Learner teachers, and Special Education teachers in all units of LETRS by July 1, 2025.
● Currently, twenty-three percent of this group has been trained in Units 1-8 of LETRS.
● RAS is also exploring new curricular resources that align with the Science of Reading, specifically using the Structured Literacy approach, which will be implemented in the next two years.
● The Curriculum and Instruction team will continue to offer ongoing professional learning, coaching, and support in all areas of literacy instruction.

Math
Where we were
It is important to look at the growth that students are making according to the screening data. The percentage of students in kindergarten and first grade demonstrating typical growth from fall to winter is similar to national growth norms which is an improvement from previous years. In second grade, students had relatively consistent growth levels over the last several years and continued to be above national growth norms. In grades three through five, more students have shown growth than in previous years but continue to be slightly under national growth norms. There continue to be below-average percentages of students making growth as compared to national norms as students move from primary to intermediate grade levels.
Where we are
As students progress through each grade, more memorization of steps is needed due to a heavy focus on procedural skills. There tends to be an overemphasis on procedural thinking and memorization rather than conceptually understanding mathematical concepts. It is difficult for students to retain the information while memorizing steps rather than applying conceptual mathematical understanding, which results in a superficial understanding. This leads to significant time spent on reteaching previous grade-level content and less time on grade-level standards. In addition, with limited time during the day, there needs to be more classroom time dedicated to covering all of the grade-level standards.
RAS employs a variety of reading interventions to meet the needs of students:
● Number sense interventions
● FASTBridge interventions
● IXL
● Redbird
● WIN time interventions
Where we are going
An in-depth examination of Tier 1 instruction shows a need to support teachers in developing a deeper conceptual understanding of mathematics. To better meet the needs of students and build conceptual understanding:
● Additional methods of modeling problem-solving in math instruction will be utilized that allow for student thinking rather than memorization.
● Additional professional development, modeling, and coaching will be provided to meet that need.
● The focus of professional development will be changing mathematics instruction from a direct instruction model to a balanced model of direct instruction and inquiry-based learning.
● RAS has developed a plan to dedicate time and space for educators to read their grade level standards four times a year to thoroughly understand the scope and sequence of their grade level math instruction.
● To meet the appropriate level of rigor, student coursework will be examined and compared with the benchmark achievement level descriptors provided by MDE.
● A strong foundation of conceptual understanding and grade level standards is a priority focus.

FastBridge Results: Elementary Schools
The district administers FastBridge assessments to monitor the academic achievement of students. This report to the school board summarizes the results of math and reading assessments for elementary-aged students for the winter of 2024. Students in kindergarten and first grade took earlyReading and earlyMath assessments in the fall of 2023 and again in the winter of 2024. Students in grades two through five took aReading and aMath assessments during the same time periods.
The earlyReading and earlyMath assessments are individually administered assessments. Students use paper-based materials and the test administrator (teacher) marks student responses on an electronic device.
The earlyReading assessment covers skills such as concepts of print, phonemic awareness (onset sounds, word segmenting), phonics (letter names, letter sounds, nonsense word fluency), and reading fluency (sight words and sentence reading). The earlyMath assessment covers early numeracy skills such as recognizing numerals, recognizing quantities, matching numerals to quantities, and solving verbally presented word problems. The specific skills assessed vary depending on the grade level and season of the assessment.
The aReading and aMath assessments are computer-adaptive tests that measure broad reading and math skills.

Fall to Winter Growth
Growth Definition
The district also monitors the percentage of students making Typical Growth. In this report, students are considered making Typical Growth if their fall-to-winter growth is at the 40th percentile or better. Growth percentiles are basically a ranking of student growth. If a student is at the 40th percentile for growth, it means the student grew the same or more than forty percent of students in that grade. As one reviews Typical Growth data within this report, it is helpful to note that having 60% of students making Typical Growth is equivalent to the national average.
