Executive Summary: K-5 ELA Curriculum Review
Robbinsdale Area Schools | May 2025
In alignment with the Minnesota READ Act, Robbinsdale Area Schools conducted a comprehensive review of K-5 English Language Arts (ELA) curricular resources to ensure alignment with the Science of Reading and Structured Literacy. This curriculum review included extensive engagement from classroom teachers, administrators, special education staff, English learner educators, community members, and literacy experts. The process culminated in a districtwide recommendation to adopt Wit & Wisdom as the new
K-5 ELA knowledge-building resource and Functional Phonics & Morphology as the foundational resource for grades 4 & 5, beginning in the 2025-26 school year.
Overview of Legislative Requirements
Under the Minnesota READ Act, districts must adopt evidence-based high-quality literacy resources that support student mastery in five key areas: phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary development, reading fluency, and reading comprehension. Districts may only use state-provided Literacy Aid to purchase resources from a vetted list approved by the
Minnesota Department of Education (MDE) and CAREI. Robbinsdale already has a highly aligned foundational resource (Bridge2Read) for grades K-3. The district's current review focused on identifying a highly aligned knowledge-building or comprehensive resource to support full K-5 implementation.
Curriculum Review Process
The review followed a transparent and inclusive process:
● Dec 2024 - April 2025: Literacy Committee formed with representatives from K-5,
EL, SPED, administration, and instructional leaders.
● March - April 2025: Vendor meetings, demos, special education literacy team review, and principal input sessions.
● May 2, 2025: Vendor fair for all elementary staff and community members.
● Resources Reviewed:
○ EL Education (K-5) - Comprehensive curriculum

○ Wit & Wisdom (K-5) - Knowledge-building curriculum

Instructional Considerations and Final Recommendation
✔ Why Wit & Wisdom Was Recommended:
● Strong alignment with vocabulary, comprehension, and language development.
● Includes rich student materials and supports for academic oral and written language.
● Clear guidance for multilingual learners, including embedded supports and language dives.

● Emphasizes rigorous, content-integrated instruction with connections to science and social studies.
● Strong equity lens with accurate, diverse text selections.
● Endorsed by multiple districts and supported by research that shows positive, if moderate, gains with high fidelity implementation.
✔ Considerations Regarding EL Education:
● Preferred by special education staff due to its predictable structure, built-in scaffolds, and progress monitoring tools.
● Better fit for resource and co-taught classrooms.
● Strong engagement and discourse opportunities were noted, but they are time-intensive for teachers and less effective in grades 3-5 without strong foundational supports.
● Research indicates substantial gains in early grades and with high implementation support.
✔ Research & Evidence:
● Both curricula have research to support their effectiveness. CAREI provided substantial reviews of both curricula.
● Wit & Wisdom is associated with steady improvement in literacy outcomes in some districts, but results vary by grade and context.
● Research across both options emphasizes the importance of implementation fidelity and professional learning.
● In discussions with districts currently implementing Wit & Wisdom, including
Anoka-Hennepin and South St. Paul, leaders reported that the materials are highly engaging for their students. They observed that students interact more authentically with texts and demonstrate deeper engagement. However, some concerns were raised about the level of scaffolding provided-specifically, that much of the instructional support is left to the teacher to develop, making preparation time-consuming. Despite this, overall feedback on the program is positive, and district leaders are eager to review student data after several years of implementation.
● In discussions with the Shakopee and Bloomington districts, which have recently begun using Wit & Wisdom, leaders noted increased student engagement. They highlighted that writing and student discourse are well-integrated into the curriculum. However, they expressed concerns that the foundational skills component does not fully meet their students' needs. Additionally, they noted that lesson preparation is time-consuming.

Conclusion & Next Steps
Based on stakeholder input, legislative alignment, instructional quality, and long-term implementation considerations, Robbinsdale Area Schools recommends the adoption of Wit & Wisdom as the district's K-5 ELA knowledge-building resource and
Functional Phonics & Morphology as the foundational resource for grades 4 and 5.
The decision balances the district's goals for academic rigor, equity, multilingual learner success, and representation of diverse voices, particularly for American Indian students.
Next steps include:
● Planning comprehensive professional development for all K-5 staff
● Integrating differentiation tools for special education settings
● Continuing to evaluate the effectiveness of implementation across all subgroups
